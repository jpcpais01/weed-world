## 24 Oct 2026 · 23:56 UTC
- **Smoother in thick forest on PC.** Looking into dense trees, the graphics card was the limit, and 2× MSAA anti-aliasing added extra depth work for every layer of leaves while leaving the cut-out leaf edges jagged anyway. The High preset on PC now uses SMAA, which smooths the leaf edges too for a small fixed cost. MSAA 2× and 4× are still in the Anti-aliasing setting (Ultra keeps 4×), and phones keep MSAA, which costs them almost nothing.
- **Time the anti-aliasing.** "Measure parts" in the frame-time panel now includes anti-aliasing. It waits a few seconds for the new shaders to build so the number is real. The panel's first line also calls a frame GPU-bound once the graphics card is 80% busy.

## 24 Oct 2026 · 21:00 UTC
- **A quarter fewer draw calls.** The processor spent the most time telling the graphics card what to draw. Rocks no longer cast shadows past 35 m (big boulders still cast further), where they were a few blurry pixels yet each patch of them cost a draw call. On PC, trees past 40 m now cast their shadow as a single card, as phones already did. In side-by-side renders the shadows look the same, with about 40% fewer shadow draws.
- **No loading in the middle of play.** Music now loads in the background before a tune starts, and bird and frog calls load once instead of every time one sings, so neither can make a frame stall.
- **Find the stutters.** The frame-time panel now picks out your slowest recent frame and splits it into scripts, physics (and how many physics steps it ran), and drawing and waiting, naming the busiest system in it. The first line now says what really limits the frame rate, including when neither the processor nor the graphics card is full and the time goes on a few slow frames.

## 24 Oct 2026 · 20:02 UTC
- **About twice as fast on PC.** Windows now draws with Godot's Forward+ renderer, which works out what's in front before painting, so the thick leafy crowns no longer get painted over and over. On an RTX 4060 laptop at 1440p the graphics card went from 9.3 to 4.7 ms a frame (trees 6.4 to 3.3 ms, shadows 3.7 to 3.1 ms) with the same look. Phones keep the Mobile renderer.
- **Which graphics card?** The frame-time panel now shows the graphics card the game is using, whether it's the dedicated or built-in one, the driver and the renderer.
- **No more popping around you.** Grass and shadows near your Keling used to swap their detail level as the camera swung round you, so things seemed to "load" or flicker right next to you. Looking around now changes nothing, the swaps only happen as you walk, each patch holds its detail until well past the edge (no flipping back and forth), and the grass and leafy shadow swaps happen further away.

## 24 Oct 2026 · 18:40 UTC
- **Lighter tree shadows to draw.** The evening sun shines low through the forest, and the big leafy trees were casting their shadows from every single leaf, about 35 layers deep, so the graphics card painted each spot of the shadow map around a dozen times. Trees now cast from just enough leaves to block the sun: the shadows look the same (up close the dappling lets through about 3% more light; further away the shadows are actually fuller than before) and the shadow work drops by about 40%.

## 24 Oct 2026 · 16:50 UTC
- **No more flickering.** Trees, bushes and grass no longer flash or jump for a frame while you walk or turn the camera. The forest was smoothing its own detail swaps between frames, which briefly drew plants in the wrong spot; it now swaps them cleanly.
- **Lighter leafy views.** Looking across the forest costs the graphics card less: nearby plants are drawn before the ones behind them, and leaves on each tree are drawn from the outside in, so hidden leaves are skipped instead of painted over and over.

Tap the panel (or press 9) and it measures what each part of the world costs to draw (shadows, trees, grass, water, sky and more) by switching them off one at a time for a moment. ## 24 Oct 2026 · 16:05 UTC
- **A much lighter forest.** The Whispering Glade now draws well under half the triangles it used to from any spot in the valley, so it runs smoother on phones and laptops alike, without losing its look. Every tree, bush, flower and tuft of grass now picks its own level of detail by how far it is from you, instead of whole patches of forest switching at once, and plants stop drawing the moment they have faded away.

- **Alive in every frame.** Breathing, head turns, leaning into corners, feet that settle on slopes, and a crown of stalks and a basket that bounce as you move.
- **Play your way.** Touch joystick and buttons on phones (push the stick past its ring to sprint), plus keyboard, mouse and gamepad. Try the emotes: dance, cheer, sit and nod.
- **Test grounds.** An open meadow with jump blocks, ramps, a tower to drop from, a slalom and a bumpy slope to try every move.
